# Project Rationale — Parkdale Parent Resources Project

## What this project is

The Parent Resources Project is a **resource curation and communication project**. Its purpose is to give Parkdale families easy access to reliable, respectful, and accessible information that supports children's sense of belonging, identity, cultural understanding, and family inclusion — and to communicate candidate resources clearly to Anna for her decision. It is not a finished display, and it does not provide clinical or legal advice.

## Why LGBTQ+ and Indigenous resources are relevant to family engagement

Parkdale serves a diverse community, including families with gender-diverse children and Indigenous families. Including well-sourced, professionally produced materials on these topics signals to all families that the centre values their identity and experience, and gives parents accessible starting points for conversations they may not otherwise have support for. This directly supports children's sense of belonging and family engagement — core goals of early childhood education.

## Why supervisor review is required, and what Jiecheng is (and isn't) deciding

These topics can be sensitive, and the resource-selection process must prioritize accuracy, age-appropriateness, and fit with Parkdale's specific community before anything is displayed. As a student intern, Jiecheng's role is to research, screen, and organize candidate resources — **not to make final content or print decisions for the parent resource area.** Every resource summary, the review table, and the bookstore wish list are suggestions only. All materials require Anna's approval before printing, purchasing, or placement. Anna's review ensures professional oversight, consistency with centre policy, and an additional check against any content that may need adjustment before reaching families.

## Connection to inclusion, belonging, cultural responsiveness, and professional responsibility

This project supports several things at once: **family belonging** (children and families seeing their identities reflected and respected), **inclusive communication** (presenting resources to Anna in a clear, organized, low-pressure way rather than acting unilaterally), **Indigenous learning** (introducing reconciliation-related material thoughtfully, through age-appropriate, professionally produced sources), and **professional responsibility** (working from official/professional sources rather than assumptions, and recognizing the limits of a student intern's authority to make final placement decisions). Submitting this package for supervisor approval — rather than printing, displaying, or purchasing materials independently — demonstrates professional accountability and respect for the supervised nature of the internship.
