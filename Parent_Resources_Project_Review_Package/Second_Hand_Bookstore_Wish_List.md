# Second-Hand Bookstore Wish List

Based on: Anna's verbal idea (Tuesday) about looking for suitable books from second-hand bookstores for the Parkdale Parent Resources area.

Status: Suggested categories and search guidance only — for Anna's review and feedback. No books have been purchased, borrowed, or added to the parent resource area yet. Anna's approval is needed before sourcing or displaying any titles.

## How to use this list

This is a browsing guide for second-hand bookstores, not a confirmed shopping list. Anna should review the categories and keywords below, adjust them as needed, and confirm which categories (if any) are worth pursuing before Jiecheng or anyone else starts searching shelves.

## 1. Indigenous children's books

- What to look for: Picture books by Indigenous authors/illustrators (own-voices), age-appropriate stories about land, family, community, and identity; books published by Indigenous-led or reputable Canadian publishers (e.g., Theytus Books, Highwater Press, Annick Press Indigenous titles).
- What to avoid: Books written by non-Indigenous authors that stereotype or romanticize Indigenous cultures; outdated or inaccurate depictions; anything treating Residential Schools or trauma without age-appropriate framing.
- Why it may support Parkdale families: Gives young children early, respectful exposure to Indigenous stories and presence, supporting reconciliation goals in an age-appropriate way.
- Example keywords: "own voices Indigenous picture book," "First Nations children's story," "Cindy Blackstock Spirit Bear," "Indigenous picture book Canada."

## 2. Diverse family books

- What to look for: Picture books showing a range of family structures (single parents, grandparent-led families, adoptive families, multi-generational households, blended families).
- What to avoid: Books that present one family type as "normal" and others as exceptions; outdated illustrations or language.
- Why it may support Parkdale families: Helps every child see a family that looks like theirs reflected in the classroom, supporting belonging.
- Example keywords: "diverse families picture book," "all kinds of families," "family structures children's book."

## 3. LGBTQ+ family picture books

- What to look for: Simple, warm picture books featuring same-sex parents or gender-diverse characters, written for very young children, with age-appropriate language.
- What to avoid: Books aimed at older children/teens; books with complex political framing rather than simple family depiction; unofficial or self-published books with no quality/editorial review.
- Why it may support Parkdale families: Supports children from 2SLGBTQ+ families feeling represented, and introduces all children to family diversity early — pending Anna's confirmation this fits the centre's approach.
- Example keywords: "two dads picture book," "two moms picture book," "gender diverse children's book," "rainbow family picture book."

## 4. Newcomer and multicultural family stories

- What to look for: Picture books about moving to a new country, learning a new language, or celebrating cultural traditions from around the world; bilingual or dual-language books.
- What to avoid: Books that focus only on struggle/hardship without warmth or belonging; stereotypical portrayals of specific cultures.
- Why it may support Parkdale families: Reflects newcomer and multicultural families in the program and supports children adjusting to a new community.
- Example keywords: "newcomer children's book," "immigrant family picture book," "bilingual picture book," "multicultural children's story."

## 5. Emotion, identity, and belonging books

- What to look for: Simple books that help young children name feelings, understand who they are, and feel they belong in a group.
- What to avoid: Books with heavy or abstract language not suited to early childhood; books that moralize rather than tell a story.
- Why it may support Parkdale families: Builds social-emotional skills and a sense of belonging, both core early-years goals.
- Example keywords: "feelings picture book toddler," "belonging children's book," "identity picture book preschool."

## 6. Kindness, empathy, and community books

- What to look for: Picture books about sharing, helping others, friendship, and being part of a community or classroom.
- What to avoid: Books with confusing morals or content above toddler/preschool comprehension level.
- Why it may support Parkdale families: Reinforces the centre's everyday social skills curriculum and gives parents read-along material that matches classroom values.
- Example keywords: "kindness picture book," "empathy children's book," "community helpers story," "friendship picture book preschool."

## 7. Parenting support books

- What to look for: Practical, accessible parenting books from recognized publishers or organizations, covering topics like early childhood development, supporting a gender-diverse child, or talking to children about difficult topics.
- What to avoid: Self-help books with no credentials behind them, books making clinical/medical claims, or anything that reads as advice Parkdale staff should not be seen endorsing without Anna's sign-off.
- Why it may support Parkdale families: Gives parents accessible starting points for conversations at home, consistent with the project's overall goal of supporting (not replacing) professional guidance.
- Example keywords: "early childhood parenting guide," "supporting your child book," "parenting resource library book."

## Notes

- This list only covers categories and search direction — it does not name specific titles, since availability at second-hand bookstores varies and titles still need individual review for fit before purchase.
- Any books actually found should be logged for Anna's review the same way online resources are, before being added to the Parkdale Parent Resources area.
