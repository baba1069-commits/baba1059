# Keenan (2017) — Unscripting Curriculum: Toward a Critical Trans Pedagogy

- **Author:** [[Harper_B_Keenan]]
- **Citation:** Keenan, H. B. (2017). "Unscripting Curriculum: Toward a Critical Trans Pedagogy." *Harvard Educational Review*, 87(4), 538–556.
- **Source:** Harvard Educational Review official page (harvardeducationalreview.org/content/87/4/538) and ERIC (EJ1164883).
- **Themes:** [[LGBTQ_Family_Resources]], [[Early_Childhood_Education]]

## What the article argues (from the official abstract)

Keenan draws on his own experience as a white queer and trans educator and former elementary school teacher to examine what a "critical trans pedagogy" means in practice. He explores how his own classroom teaching was shaped both by his personal experience of gender conditioning and by the political climate around trans identity at the time. His central claim: a critical trans pedagogy requires **"unscripting"** — moving away from pre-set, adult-determined narratives about gender — and must actively support children in constructing their own knowledge about gender, rather than simply receiving adult-approved narratives.

## Why this matters for Jiecheng's pathway

- This is a **classroom-practice-level** argument, not abstract theory — Keenan writes from his own experience as a former elementary educator, closer to Jiecheng's own ECE/RECE practitioner standpoint than most academic education research.
- The "unscripting" concept is a useful, citable academic frame for [[Parkdale_Parent_Resources_Project]]: curating LGBTQ+ family resources for parents is one step removed from Keenan's child-facing classroom focus, but both concern how adults introduce children to ideas about gender and family rather than scripting one approved narrative.
- Useful contrast for a personal statement: Keenan's article is about **direct classroom pedagogy with children**; Jiecheng's project is about **curating resources for parents**, with supervisor (Anna) approval as a gate. Adjacent but distinct levels of practice — worth being precise about this rather than overstating the overlap.
