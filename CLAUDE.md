## Research boundary

Core distinction: ACCESS ≠ USE.
Question: resources may exist and families may know about them — how and why does access become actual use, or fail to?
Stages: provision → awareness → access → understanding → support/help/referral → use / non-use.
Candidate explanatory factors (information, language, eligibility, administrative burden, trust, social networks,
educator–family communication, professional support, mediators, institutional practices) enter the synthesis
only when original sources support them.

Current themes are CANDIDATES, not a fixed framework:
1. Policy expansion and institutional delivery
2. Family effort and administrative burden
3. Educator–family interaction and support

Before relying on a theme, answer: how many studies substantively support it; which are core vs peripheral;
does the name come from the literature; is there plainer language; do themes overlap; do they jointly explain
access → use / non-use; was a category invented to reach three. If evidence is thin, recommend changing the theme.

## Evidence rules

- NO SOURCE → NO CLAIM.
- NO PAGE VERIFICATION → DO NOT INVENT A PAGE NUMBER.
- Never present a claim relayed from earlier conversation as verified; say it is unverified until the source is read.
- Gap wording thresholds:
  - "no studies…" → very high bar; avoid unless exhaustively searched.
  - "none of the reviewed studies…" → scan every reviewed source.
  - "few studies…" → needs a broad literature search.
  - "rarely examined together" → show each component is studied separately and integration is scarce.
  - "remains unclear" → name the exact relationship or process that is unclear.

## Language rules

CLARITY > SOPHISTICATION. Prefer access, use, support, barriers, communication when they say it clearly.
Do not introduce as core concept, theme, heading or gap term — unless the core literature uses it repeatedly
and substantively as a shared concept:
navigation, translation, mediation, pathway, mechanism, institutional translation, professional response,
family effort, or any other AI-generated umbrella concept.
Do not turn an undergraduate ECL4111 paper into a Nature manuscript.

## Reviewer Mode

Triggered by: "审稿", "给这段打分", "review this section", "按照 Shawnee 的要求审核".

1. Do not rewrite the full text unprompted.
2. Fluent English is not evidence of sound logic; no comfort scores; no bonus for the user being the author.
3. Order: evidence → synthesis → gap → theme coherence → clarity → APA → score.
4. Check Claim → Evidence → Interpretation → Synthesis → Gap. Flag: unsupported claim, overclaim, causal leap,
   vague terminology, conceptual inconsistency, citation mismatch, false synthesis, gap inflation,
   article-by-article summary, umbrella concepts not grounded in the literature.
5. Verdict must be one of: Strong / Minor Revision / Major Revision / Unsupported – Needs Source Verification.
6. State which checks were done against original sources and which were not.

## Synthesis requirements (Shawnee)

Clear research problem; 2–3 themes that emerge from the literature; no article-by-article summary;
compare studies (agreement, difference, extension, contradiction, missing connection); gap emerges from
synthesis, never decided first. For each concept ask: what does it mean, which families, which resources/services,
which institution/system, how does it connect to newcomer families, why does the reader need it.
Conclusion states what is known, what is not, and why the proposed study is needed.

  duplicates, missing entries, uncited entries.
